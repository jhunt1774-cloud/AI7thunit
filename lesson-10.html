First:read today’s explanation

A finished computing artifact needs an explanation of its decisions and limits. Lantern Reach is one class project, but each designer can show individual thinking through a before/after example, code, tests, and a justified revision. A polished image alone does not reveal who understood the algorithm or who checked a result.

We will create a release package: the editable scene saved by the teacher, an image or labeled map for viewing, a change log, test results, and credits. A .blend file preserves editable scene data; a PNG image is a view, not an editable 3D replacement. Our original drawings need creator credit, and any external resources would need their source and permission checked. AI assistance should be described honestly, without treating AI output as proof that no outside rights are involved.

Public sharing is a separate decision. Student names, account details, and private classroom records do not belong in a public model or AI prompt. Use a class project title or anonymous design IDs. Before a release, compare the final suite with the charter and label remaining unknowns. If rendering fails, a viewport capture or paper map can still support a strong explanation. The most valuable final sentence may be a limitation: what evidence would you need before making a stronger claim?

Open Day 10 six-question check

Finished early? Choose one.

These are extra choices after your required work;your teacher can assign one in place of waiting work. Read the finished-product requirement before you start.

Choice A · suggested 10–18 minutes; move on when evidence is complete

Alternative design defense

Write a counterproposal replacing the bridge loop with explicit calls or changing the grove skip rule. Trace the alternative, compare two metrics, and identify one test it fails or improves. Write a150-word defense that includes evidence supporting the original design as well as your alternative.

Turn in: Alternative algorithm, trace, metric comparison, balanced defense.

Choice B · suggested 10–18 minutes; move on when evidence is complete

Future maintainer challenge

Write a handoff for next year’s class: module inventory with six entries, coordinate convention, naming rules, three known risks, and a five-step safe change procedure. Give it to a partner who must answer four questions without your help: tower height, river bounds, gate minimum, and where tests are stored. Revise missing information.

Turn in: Maintainer guide, four retrieval answers, revised handoff.

Further challenge

Evidence cross-examination

Write three claims you could defend and three claims you cannot yet defend, such as universal accessibility or a guaranteed processing time. For each unsupported claim design the next test, identify needed participants/data, and state a possible disconfirming result.

Turn in: Your supported explanation,trace,or revised plan.

If technology fails

Present paper portfolios and the shared map. Archive photos or teacher notes later if available. Accept a written or recorded explanation in an accessible mode; do not require a live render, personal account, or public upload.
