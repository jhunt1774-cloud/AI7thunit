Learn this before doing today’s work

How to use this section: read the six explanations,follow the worked examples,then try the two guided questions. Your teacher will discuss them before assigning independent work. Return to the numbered explanation if you need help with a check question.

1. Center, height, top, and bottom

The center is halfway between an object’s top and bottom. For height h and center z=c, bottom=c-h/2 and top=c+h/2. To rest on ground g, center=g+h/2. Half-height is needed because the object extends both above and below its center. These rules describe our simple upright bodies, not arbitrary rotated meshes.

2. Footprint edges use half the width

A footprint is the top-down area an object occupies. For center x and width w, left edge=x-w/2 and right edge=x+w/2. Do the same with y and depth. Checking only a center can miss an edge crossing a river or the terrain boundary. Write negative numbers carefully: -6-2=-8, while -6+2=-4.

3. Our coordinate convention

Coordinates are ordered x,y,z. On our planning map x increases east, y increases north, and z increases upward. Ground top is z=0. The tower center (-7,4,4) means west of the origin, north of the origin, and 4 units upward. A minus sign is a direction relative to the origin, not automatically an invalid position.

4. A precise prompt supplies executable detail

A prompt should say what to keep, which named objects to create or update, dimensions, center positions, and what to report. “Tall and nearby” leaves height and distance undecided. Natural-language instructions can still be misunderstood, so include acceptance tests. A clear request reduces ambiguity but never eliminates the need to inspect the result.

5. Separate requirement, prediction, and observation

A requirement is what we want. A prediction is what we expect the instructions to produce. An observation is what we actually inspect or measure. An unverified AI statement is a claim, even if it repeats our desired number. Put unknown in the result column when suitable evidence is unavailable; do not copy the requirement into that column and call it measured.

6. A separate roof needs its own center

When a roof of height r sits on a body top t, roof center=t+r/2. Roof bottom must equal t for contact. For our simple body, t=ground+body_height. A roof centered at the body top would extend partly inside the body. Record roof bottom and top to distinguish contact, gap, and overlap.

Watch how it is done:worked examples

Ground a different tower

Body height 6, ground 0: half-height=3, center=3, bottom=3-3=0, top=3+3=6. Put a roof of height 4 on it: roof center=6+2=8; roof bottom=6; roof top=10. Each center is calculated, not guessed from the view.

Find the footprint and a raised-ground center

A body centered at (-6,3) with width 4 and depth 2 has x edges -8,-4 and y edges 2,4. If its height is 6 and ground is g=2, center z=2+3=5. Its bottom is 5-3=2, exactly the raised ground.

Try together before working independently

Write or say your answer first. Then open the explanation and compare your method,not only your final value.

Guided practice 1

An upright box is 10 high and must rest on ground 0. Find center, bottom, and top.

Compare your answer and reasoning

Half-height=5, center=5, bottom=0, top=10. The reason is center ± half-height, not “z always equals height.”

Guided practice 2

A roof is 2 high and sits on body top 10. What is its center? What would go wrong with center 10?

Compare your answer and reasoning

Center=10+1=11. Center 10 would put its bottom at 9, overlapping the body by 1.

Help for the worksheet and extra choices

Diagram help: use one top view for x/y edges and one side view for z values. A margin means empty space between an object edge and the terrain edge; a one-unit margin requires more than merely staying on the boundary. For the fast-finisher formula, replace ground0 with g everywhere: body center=g+h/2;roof center=g+h+r/2.

Connect the idea to our class world

Our world uses a shared coordinate system. On the top-down map, x increases east and y increases north. In Blender, z increases upward. Ground level is z=0. A location is an object’s center unless the contract says otherwise. This matters: a box of height 8 centered at z=0 extends from z=-4 to z=4. To rest its bottom on the ground, its center must be at z=4.

The tower body has a 4-by-4 footprint, height 8, and center (-7,4,4). Its x edges are -9 and -5; its y edges are 2 and 6. The roof is a separate cone with height 2 centered at (-7,4,9), so its bottom meets the body’s top at z=8. Units are classroom world units, not a claim about real construction.

A prompt is natural language, so it can contain ambiguities. An algorithm needs ordered, sufficiently precise actions. “Make a tall tower nearby” leaves height, reference point, and distance open. A stronger request names the objects, values, preservation rules, and tests. Predictions must come before inspection: otherwise we may accidentally describe whatever the AI made as if that was our original goal. We also distinguish a desired measurement from a measured result. Until checked, a dimension is a requirement.
