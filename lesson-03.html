Target: Specify a tower using coordinates, dimensions, and measurable acceptance tests.

Work in your paper journal or a school-approved document. The times are planning ranges, not timers to wait out. Finish the evidence, then choose another card.

Start here · 2–3 minutes

Center or bottom?

A 6-unit-high box is centered at z=1. Find top and bottom. Then find the center needed to place its bottom at ground.

Student reading · 3–5 minutes

Our world uses a shared coordinate system. On the top-down map, x increases east and y increases north. In Blender, z increases upward. Ground level is z=0. A location is an object’s center unless the contract says otherwise. This matters: a box of height 8 centered at z=0 extends from z=-4 to z=4. To rest its bottom on the ground, its center must be at z=4.

The tower body has a 4-by-4 footprint, height 8, and center (-7,4,4). Its x edges are -9 and -5; its y edges are 2 and 6. The roof is a separate cone with height 2 centered at (-7,4,9), so its bottom meets the body’s top at z=8. Units are classroom world units, not a claim about real construction.

A prompt is natural language, so it can contain ambiguities. An algorithm needs ordered, sufficiently precise actions. “Make a tall tower nearby” leaves height, reference point, and distance open. A stronger request names the objects, values, preservation rules, and tests. Predictions must come before inspection: otherwise we may accidentally describe whatever the AI made as if that was our original goal. We also distinguish a desired measurement from a measured result. Until checked, a dimension is a requirement.

Core design work · 11–16 minutes

Required evidence; use partner feedback after your own draft

Tower specification laboratory

Draw top and side views of the tower described in the reading. Label all three center coordinates, four footprint edges, body top, roof bottom, and roof top. Rewrite “Make a tall blue tower near the river” as an ordered 6-step request using names Tower_body and Tower_roof. Include a preservation rule and four acceptance tests. Partner acts as a literal executor and marks three possible ambiguities; revise your own request and predict the resulting object count.

Turn in: Two labeled diagrams; six-step request; four tests; three feedback notes with revisions.

While the teacher computer works · 5–9 minutes

Independent; start immediately at the teacher signal

Prompt comparison

Compare A: “Build a tower, very tall, beside everything.” B: “Create Tower_body, dimensions 4×4×8, center (-7,4,4), preserve terrain; report dimensions.” List four unknowns in A. Explain two strengths and two missing details in B. Write your improved version and predict its bounds.

Turn in: Eight annotations, improved prompt, predicted bounds.

Choice board · 10–18 minutes each

Choose a new card when required evidence is complete. If you finish a card, select the other card or the challenge. Quality checks, traces, and revisions are part of the work.

Choice A · 10–18 minutes

Collision review

Test tower proposals P=(-7,4), Q=(0,4), R=(10,8), all with a 4×4 footprint. The river occupies -2<x<2; terrain edges are x=±12,y=±10. Draw footprints, identify river overlap or boundary contact, and recommend a placement with a one-unit terrain-edge margin. Explain why checking centers alone can miss overlap.

Turn in: Three footprints, six boundary checks, and a recommendation.

Choice B · 10–18 minutes

Literal partner challenge

Write a second tower specification with height 10, width 3, at (-7,-3). Do not show your drawing to a partner. Have them sketch from your words; compare six measurements. Revise the two least clear instructions and explain whether each mismatch was a wording error or execution error.

Turn in: Two independently produced drawings, six comparisons, and two diagnosed revisions.

Fast-finisher challenge · 7–12 minutes

General grounding rule

Derive a formula for center z using ground height g and body height h. Use g=2,h=7, then g=-1,h=4. Explain how a separate roof’s center depends on g,h, and roof height r.

Turn in: A complete challenge response with calculations or evidence.

No computer? Keep designing.

Students use graph paper and the numeric tower report supplied in the reading. Add a paper tower marker to the shared map. Compare predictions with the written report and label it a simulated result. No live view or render is needed.

Choose your grade target

  • Grade 6: Use a coordinate word bank and complete body center/top/bottom; write four precise steps.
  • Grade 7: Complete both views, the six-step request, and all four tests.
  • Grade 8: Derive grounding formulas, check footprint bounds, and distinguish requirement, prediction, and measurement.

Evidence check before you leave

  1. Label your work with lesson number and anonymous design ID.
  2. Separate requirements, predictions, observations, and simulated results.
  3. Mark your revision and explain why you made it.
  4. Turn in the required output even if your proposal was not selected.
