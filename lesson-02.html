Target: Break the shared world into modules with explicit dependencies and tests.

Work in your paper journal or a school-approved document. The times are planning ranges, not timers to wait out. Finish the evidence, then choose another card.

Start here · 2–3 minutes

Parts or decorations?

List six parts of a fantasy village. Underline the three whose placement could affect a traveler’s route. Explain one choice.

Student reading · 3–5 minutes

A large problem becomes easier to manage when we decompose it into smaller parts. Lantern Reach contains a tower module, a bridge module, a home module, a grove module, and a gate module. Each module has inputs, work, and outputs. A tower may take a position and height as inputs and produce a body and roof as outputs. Decorative wishes become useful when they are attached to a specific module and a test.

Some modules depend on others. The terrain must have a known surface before a tower’s ground contact can be checked. The river corridor must be known before the bridge can be tested. A dependency arrow means “must be ready before,” not simply “looks near.” Independent tasks can be planned at the same time even though only the teacher executes Blender changes.

Abstraction means keeping the details needed for the current decision. A top-down rectangle can stand for a house when we are planning routes; roof shingles do not help that decision. Later, a roof shape may matter. We will preserve stable object names so that a later instruction changes a known module instead of accidentally replacing the whole world. Breaking a world into nouns is only a start. To support a program, each part also needs responsibilities, inputs, and tests.

Core design work · 11–16 minutes

Required evidence; use partner feedback after your own draft

Module contract board

Create five module cards: tower, river/bridge, homes, grove, gate. Each card needs two inputs, two outputs or responsibilities, and two acceptance tests. Draw dependencies among the cards and terrain. Write a valid build order of six steps. Partner-check by asking whether any step uses an unknown input; revise two cards. Individually explain which roof details you omitted from the route plan and why.

Turn in: Five completed cards; dependency diagram; six-step order; abstraction explanation.

While the teacher computer works · 5–9 minutes

Independent; start immediately at the teacher signal

Dependency detective

Audit this order: bridge, river, tower roof, terrain, tower body, homes. Identify three ordering problems; produce a corrected order. For each correction write what information was missing and a consequence of building early.

Turn in: Three error/cause/consequence rows and one corrected order.

Choice board · 10–18 minutes each

Choose a new card when required evidence is complete. If you finish a card, select the other card or the challenge. Quality checks, traces, and revisions are part of the work.

Choice A · 10–18 minutes

Two decompositions

Design a second breakdown using regions (north, south, riverfront) rather than object types. Write responsibilities for each region. Compare the two plans on duplicated work, dependency clarity, and ease of a tower-only revision. Choose a plan and defend it with two examples.

Turn in: Two diagrams and a three-criterion comparison.

Choice B · 10–18 minutes

Contract negotiation

A partner proposes a bridge 1 unit wide and a gate 3 units wide. Your access rule requires a continuous 2-unit route. Write a change request identifying the incompatible contract. Redesign three affected modules, then draft a 6-line interface checklist another group can use without asking you questions.

Turn in: Change request, three revised contracts, six-line checklist.

Fast-finisher challenge · 7–12 minutes

Hidden dependency

Add a lantern network module. Identify two dependencies and one detail it should not need to know. Revise the diagram and explain how that separation reduces future edits.

Turn in: A complete challenge response with calculations or evidence.

No computer? Keep designing.

Use five paper cards and the base map. Physically arrange the dependency order; annotate the same contracts. No scene execution is required. Keep accepted cards for the next lesson and mark the class ledger as planned, not built.

Choose your grade target

  • Grade 6: Complete tower, bridge, and home contracts with provided input/output labels; add two more using a partner.
  • Grade 7: Complete all five contracts and explain two dependency arrows.
  • Grade 8: Add submodules, a shared interface, and one deliberate abstraction; explain what changes if the river moves.

Evidence check before you leave

  1. Label your work with lesson number and anonymous design ID.
  2. Separate requirements, predictions, observations, and simulated results.
  3. Mark your revision and explain why you made it.
  4. Turn in the required output even if your proposal was not selected.
