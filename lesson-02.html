First:read today’s explanation

A large problem becomes easier to manage when we decompose it into smaller parts. Lantern Reach contains a tower module, a bridge module, a home module, a grove module, and a gate module. Each module has inputs, work, and outputs. A tower may take a position and height as inputs and produce a body and roof as outputs. Decorative wishes become useful when they are attached to a specific module and a test.

Some modules depend on others. The terrain must have a known surface before a tower’s ground contact can be checked. The river corridor must be known before the bridge can be tested. A dependency arrow means “must be ready before,” not simply “looks near.” Independent tasks can be planned at the same time even though only the teacher executes Blender changes.

Abstraction means keeping the details needed for the current decision. A top-down rectangle can stand for a house when we are planning routes; roof shingles do not help that decision. Later, a roof shape may matter. We will preserve stable object names so that a later instruction changes a known module instead of accidentally replacing the whole world. Breaking a world into nouns is only a start. To support a program, each part also needs responsibilities, inputs, and tests.

Choice A · suggested 10–18 minutes; move on when evidence is complete

Two decompositions

Design a second breakdown using regions (north, south, riverfront) rather than object types. Write responsibilities for each region. Compare the two plans on duplicated work, dependency clarity, and ease of a tower-only revision. Choose a plan and defend it with two examples.

Turn in: Two diagrams and a three-criterion comparison.

Choice B · suggested 10–18 minutes; move on when evidence is complete

Contract negotiation

A partner proposes a bridge 1 unit wide and a gate 3 units wide. Your access rule requires a continuous 2-unit route. Write a change request identifying the incompatible contract. Redesign three affected modules, then draft a 6-line interface checklist another group can use without asking you questions.

Turn in: Change request, three revised contracts, six-line checklist.

Further challenge

Hidden dependency

Add a lantern network module. Identify two dependencies and one detail it should not need to know. Revise the diagram and explain how that separation reduces future edits.

Turn in: Your supported explanation,trace,or revised plan.

If technology fails

Use five paper cards and the base map. Physically arrange the dependency order; annotate the same contracts. No scene execution is required. Keep accepted cards for the next lesson and mark the class ledger as planned, not built.
